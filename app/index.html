    Set up onceRelay for internet connections

    Between two homes, phones or countries, a direct connection fails more often than it works: mobile data and many Wi-Fi networks block it. A free relay makes it work on any network and hides your IP address. You set it up once; only the person who starts the session needs one.

    How to get a free relay (about 2 minutes)
    1. Create a free account with a TURN relay provider, for example Metered Open Relay (many providers have free plans).
    2. In its dashboard, copy the TURN server address (it looks like relay.example.com:3478), the username and the password. You don't need a "secret key".
    3. Paste them below, exactly as shown. If the provider lists several addresses, paste them all, one per line.
    4. Click Test relay.
    Temporary logins (stronger protection when sharing)

    If your relay supports temporary logins (the "TURN REST API", often called a "secret key": some paid plans have it, and so does your own coturn server), paste its secret key here. Invites then carry a login that the relay itself rejects once the time below runs out, and your password and secret key never leave your device. You can leave the password empty if you use this.

    See if your current network allows direct connections.

          Streaming

          Choose between streaming and playing your own files with Playback mode in the Playlist tab.

          1080p uses about three times as much data as 720p: roughly 3 GB per hour on both devices, and on your relay's allowance if the connection goes through it. On mobile data, stay on 720p or lower.

          This limits both what you receive and what you send: the lower of your choice and the other person's is used. The streaming device needs about 3 Mbps of upload for 720p (6 for 1080p). Streaming from Safari isn't supported; watching a stream works everywhere.
